# About PowerGIS

PowerGIS shows power-sector data on a map of the United States.

## What is this app?

A map for exploring public data by place. A new device starts with an empty workspace: add your own data, ask the Assistant, or open the **Workspace** menu (its button pulses gently while the workspace is empty) and tap **Open the sample workspace** for a **county socioeconomic score**: each county's income, education, health insurance and housing affordability (from the Census American Community Survey), each turned into a 0–100 score and averaged into one, plus each state's averages weighted by county area. Tap a county or a state to see its numbers.

The sample workspace is built the same way you'd build your own, from six ACS county **sample** tables (income, education, health insurance, home value, rent, and a dictionary of their columns) and the **built-in** **US counties** and **US states** shapes, and ten transforms in stages: clean each table, join them onto the county shapes, compute the metrics and scores, average them by state, and attach the state shapes. Opening it takes a little while the first time, as they run. It's a draft until you **Save** it (on this device, and in your account when signed in). Connecticut has no values: the ACS reports its new planning regions, while the shapes are its old counties.

## How do I use it?

- **Search** — the box at the top reads *Search in [visualization] in [All data / a column]*; pick both, then type: it searches the features' names and every text value in the rows behind them (all data, or one column you pick), and small typos are fine. Picking a match outlines every feature that has it in pink and zooms to them — e.g. a county's name, or a state's abbreviation to show all its counties. ✕ clears it.
- **Tap a county** — a panel opens with each visualization's value there and the rows behind it: on the default map, the county's metrics and scores.
- **Hover** (on a computer) — a small card shows the county and each visualization's value.
- **Datasets** — tables of data. **＋ Dataset** takes a built-in table (**US states**, **US counties**: shapes to join your data onto), a **sample** (the example ACS county tables the default map uses), a CSV from your device, a GeoJSON / Esri JSON file, or an ArcGIS link, and stores it as a table in the database (on this device for now). **Saved** lists tables already in the database, to add back or delete. ✕ takes a dataset out of the workspace; the table stays in the database.
- **Visualizations** — what's drawn. Tap **＋ Visualization** and pick a dataset, then:
  - **Shapes** — the geometry column to draw (points, lines or regions). Rows with the same shape become one feature.
  - **Name shapes by** — optional: a column whose value titles each shape in the hover card, the tap panel and Search. It doesn't affect colors.
  - **Value to show** — what colors each shape (or, for a vector column, its arrows). Several rows on one shape are combined — count, sum, most common… — which you choose in Edit.

  It tells you how many features and rows matched.
- **Geometry** — maps draw real shapes from geometry columns. GeoJSON, Esri and ArcGIS data bring theirs, and CSV cells written as WKT (`POINT (-90 35)`) or GeoJSON count too. A CSV with only latitude and longitude columns needs a transform to turn them into points (see **Transforms**). Visualizations from different datasets show together. The default map has twelve — county scores and metrics, and state averages — with the county score shown first. Tick any combination — they stack.
- **County or state codes** — a table with FIPS codes or state abbreviations has no shapes of its own. Add the built-in **US counties** or **US states** dataset and join it in a transform, e.g. `SELECT c.geometry, c.name, d.* FROM mydata d JOIN counties c ON c.fips = d.county_fips` — then draw the result's `geometry`.
- **Copy the map** — **⧉** under the map's zoom buttons copies what the map shows, with its legend, as a picture to paste into a message or a document (or saves `map.png` where the browser can't copy pictures).
- **Graph** — the app shows the graph unless you switch to **List** (it remembers your choice on this device). It shows the workspace as a flow: datasets → transforms → the datasets they make → visualizations. Tap a node to show it below the graph; tap and hold it there for its options, as in the lists. **＋ Dataset** adds a dataset (built-in, file or ArcGIS link); pick several CSV or GeoJSON files at once and each becomes its own dataset (if one fails, the others still go in and the list says why). **＋ Transform**: tap the datasets it should read, then **Next**, and write its SQL. **＋ Visualization**: tap the dataset to draw. Move around the graph by dragging it (on a phone: touch and hold, then drag — a quick swipe still scrolls the page); zoom with Shift + scroll, a trackpad pinch, or two fingers on a phone; **＋ / − / ⤢** in its corner zoom and fit it, and **⧉** copies what the graph's window shows right now (as you've moved and zoomed it) as a picture, to paste into a message or a document (where the browser can't copy pictures, it downloads `graph.png` instead). Arrows are laid out to cross as little as possible; To remove several things at once, Ctrl/⌘- or Shift-click them (on a phone tap **Select**, then tap them), then press **Delete** (or the Delete button): they come out of the workspace, never out of your data, and a dataset that a remaining transform still uses stays (it says why). **Undo**: Ctrl/⌘+Z, or **↶** next to List | Graph, takes back any change to the workspace — deletes, edits, the assistant's changes, even Discard; **↷** (or Shift+Ctrl/⌘+Z) redoes. It covers the workspace, not data a transform has already written. **↓ / →** switches the graph between flowing down and flowing left to right (remembered on this device). Colors tell the kinds apart: datasets are violet, transforms orange, visualizations green. The dot before DATASET says where a dataset is kept: **violet** in the cloud, **gray** only on this device; a **yellow ring** around it means it's a transform's output that's out of date or not made yet. A transform with an amber **↻** badge (**needs re-run**) has an input that changed since it last ran — **↻ Run all stale** re-runs every such transform, and everything that depends on them, in the right order; a dashed one (**not run yet**) has never made its dataset.
- **Select, delete, undo** — the **✎** button (in the graph's top-left corner, and at the top of the list view) opens **Select · Delete · ↶ · ↷ · ✕**. With a mouse, Ctrl/⌘-click (or Shift-click) nodes or cards to pick several — any mix of datasets, transforms and visualizations — then press **Delete**; on a phone, tap **Select**, then tap them, then **Delete**. They're taken out of the workspace, never erased (a dataset that a remaining transform still uses stays, and it says why). **↶** (Ctrl/⌘+Z) undoes any change to the workspace and **↷** (Shift+Ctrl/⌘+Z or Ctrl+Y) redoes it; **✕** closes the tools.
- **Your account** — **☰ → Sign in**. **Create account** takes an email and a password (at least 8 characters); open the link in the email we send to confirm it, then sign in. Once you're signed in the window closes and the **☰** button is outlined in **green** (and the ◉ next to your name in the menu turns green). **Forgot password?** emails a link that opens PowerGIS ready to choose a new one. Signed in, the menu shows your name: tap it for **Your profile** — change your display name, **Change password** (type your current one, then the new one twice), **Sign out**, or **Delete account** (type your password; it deletes your saved workspaces and the datasets you own — people you shared them with lose them too — and can't be undone; data kept on this device isn't touched). **Without an account** your workspace and datasets live only in this browser on this device: clearing the browser's data, private browsing or a new device starts over, and browsers may also clear a site's stored data on their own. **With an account**, **Save** also keeps your workspace in your account: sign in on another device and it opens there (the Workspace panel says *All changes saved on this device and in your account*). Each device knows whether it matches your account: **↑** saved here but not in your account yet (Send it), **↓** another device saved a newer one (Open your account's), **↑↓** both changed since they last matched — choose **Keep this one** (it replaces your account's) or **Open your account's**. A Save never silently replaces a newer save from another device. Built-in tables and transform outputs are rebuilt on that device — the first time can take a moment while transforms run — but datasets you uploaded stay on the device you uploaded them from for now, and can't be used elsewhere yet: the Workspace panel lists them by name, with any transforms that couldn't run (and why) and what waits on them. **Nothing is removed**: missing datasets and everything that depends on them stay in the workspace, outlined in **red** in the graph (⚠ on the missing dataset itself) and marked in the lists, and they're saved along, so the device that has them still does. Tap a missing dataset in the graph and its panel opens ready to replace it: add its replacement as you would any dataset (**Cancel** closes the choice) — the replacement takes its place everywhere — then **Run them now** to remake what depends on it. Or Workspace → **Remove what didn't load** takes them all out (until you Save, Discard brings them back).
- **Cloud datasets** — the app always works on copies kept on this device. Signed in, **Link to cloud** (a dataset's options) gives a dataset a cloud copy in your account, and its card says **Cloud**; built-in tables aren't linked (every device makes its own). Nothing goes to or comes from the cloud until you **sync**: running a transform, renaming or describing a linked dataset changes this device's copy, and the card shows **Cloud ↑** (changed here). When another device or person pushes a newer version, the card shows **Cloud ↓** (the app checks when you open the workspace and every minute); **Cloud ↑↓** means both changed. **Sync…** opens a list with **Push · Pull · Skip** for each dataset: **push** sends this copy to the cloud, **pull** replaces this copy with the cloud's. Buttons that don't apply are greyed out, with the reason (nothing to push, nothing new, or you can only read it); when both changed, it starts on Skip so you choose which wins. **Unlink from the cloud** keeps this copy as a local dataset (the cloud copy stays); **Delete from the cloud** removes the cloud copy (owner only; copies on devices stay). In the **Workspace** panel, **Link to cloud…** lists datasets that are only on this device, and **Sync all…** lists linked datasets that aren't in sync — each with its choices, then go ahead; the list then says how each went. Syncing datasets is separate from saving the workspace (Save reminds you about unpushed changes). Opening your workspace on another device pulls its linked datasets; uploads that aren't linked show as missing there, and transform outputs that aren't linked are made by running their transforms. Files can be up to 50 MB compressed.
- **Sharing** — a cloud dataset's options → **Share…** lists who has it. Type the email of someone's PowerGIS account and choose **Can pull** (they get your changes) or **Can push** (they can also send theirs, and share it). They find it in **＋ Dataset → Cloud** (signed in), which lists your cloud datasets and the ones shared with you; adding one gives them a copy of their own, linked to it, which they sync like any cloud dataset. The owner can change or remove anyone; people who can push can share it, and change or remove people who can only pull. **Leave** (your own line) takes it off your list; your copy stays on this device as a local dataset.
- **Replace a dataset** — open a dataset's **Edit** → **⇄ Replace…**, then add its replacement as you'd add any dataset (a built-in, a file, ArcGIS, or one you already have under Saved) — **Cancel** closes it. It takes the old one's place everywhere in this workspace — the transforms that read it and the visualizations that draw it — and the old one stays in your database. Like other workspace edits it waits for Save. **Run them now** remakes what reads it. A transform's output can't be replaced (the transform would overwrite it): change the transform instead.
- **Only on this device** — signed in, the Workspace panel lists the datasets you uploaded that are kept only on this device: other devices, and anyone you share with, see them as missing. Saving says so too: **Link to cloud…** there stores them in the cloud. If your account's workspace was saved from another device since you last opened it here, it opens automatically; with unsaved changes here, you're asked first.
- **Explore and set up** — the app opens in **explore**: the map first, with **Search within … over …** above it and **Export** below it. On a computer the List | Graph panel stays on the left, and whatever you open shows under it; on a phone it waits behind the vertical **Set up** bar left of the search. The page always fits the screen; only panels scroll. **⤢** (computer) or the **Set up** bar (phone) switches to **set up**: the page becomes one panel with **List | Graph** and **✕** across its top. Whatever you open — a dataset, transform or visualization, its editor, or Explore — gets its own section: on a computer List | Graph sits over a small map on the left and what you open is on the right; on a phone what you open comes under List | Graph, and both scroll above a small map fixed at the bottom. The small map starts tucked away as a **▴ Map** bar at the bottom: tap it to open the map, and **▾** at the end of the bar above it tucks it away again. Drag that bar to make the map taller or shorter. **✕** (or Esc) goes back to the map; nothing you had open is lost. When you start something new (＋ Transform, ＋ Visualization, ＋ Dataset), the page scrolls to each step for you — the graph to pick from, then the form. On a computer, drag the line between the panel and the map to make the panel wider or narrower (double-click it to reset).
- **Double-tap to open** — double-tap (double-click on a computer) a card to open it: a dataset opens **Explore**, a transform or visualization opens its editor. On visualization cards, the small **Visible / Hidden** pill shows or hides it.
- **Tap and hold for options** — datasets, visualizations and transforms are cards; tap and hold one (right-click on a computer) for its menu. **Reorder** shows ≡ handles on every card — drag them, then tap **Done**.
- **Dataset options** — **Explore** (a window to look through it with SQL: it starts as `SELECT *` over the dataset, shows 200 rows a page with Prev / Next and the total count, and you can filter, sort, group or join it with any other dataset in the workspace by its table name — tap a name to insert it; nothing is saved), **Edit** (rename and describe it — an unsaved change until you Save — see its profile and id), **Copy id** (to paste into a transform's input), **Link to cloud** / **Sync…** / **Unlink from the cloud** (signed in; see **Cloud datasets** above), **Reorder**, **Remove from workspace** (the table stays in the database) and **Delete dataset** (its rows are gone for good; not while a transform reads it).
- **Local and cloud** — each dataset card says where its rows are kept: **Local** (on this device; keep as many as it holds) or **Cloud** (cloud storage, within its storage limit). Cloud storage isn't available yet, so everything is local for now. The assistant may change or delete local datasets but never cloud ones, so move anything you'd hate to lose to the cloud.
- **Visualization options** — the **Visible / Hidden** pill on the card shows or hides it; hold for **Edit**, **Zoom to data**, **Reorder** (the top of the list draws on top) and **Delete**.
- **Edit a visualization** — choose its column, how rows on the same county are combined (count, sum, average, most common…), and its colors, colormap, transparency and fill pattern.
- **Categories** — coloring by category gives every value its own color. **Palette** picks how: 12, 24 or 60 fixed colors (they repeat if there are more values — the editor tells you), **Many** for any number (shades of 12 colors), or **Auto**, which picks the smallest that fits. If another visualization is on underneath, its colors show through — untick it, or raise the opacity.
- **Vectors (arrows)** — a column holding vectors (direction and size, like a flow) is drawn as arrows automatically. Vectors in separate east / north columns can be combined with a transform: `vector(east, north)`.
- **Transforms** — SQL that makes a new dataset from your datasets. Tap **＋ Transform**, pick the input datasets (search by name — small typos are fine — or paste a dataset id) — each is a table in the SQL by the name after **as** (renaming it renames it in the SQL too) — and write one `SELECT`. Hold a transform for **Preview** (its first rows), **Run**, **Edit** and **Delete**. In the editor, set the inputs and outputs first (one line each: **⤵** puts an input's table name into the SQL, **⇄** picks another dataset); tap an input to see its rows, or an output's **▤** to see what its SQL makes, in the **Preview** panel at the bottom of the editor (tap its header to fold it away); below, each output has its own SQL and **Preview**, and **Run** writes every output together (nothing is written if any output's SQL fails). Each output writes a new dataset or an existing one no other transform writes. Deleting a transform keeps the datasets it made; to make one updatable again, have another transform's output write it. In the editor, **Preview** shows the first rows; **Run** stores the result as a dataset and adds it to the workspace (running again updates that same dataset). Examples: `SELECT name, point(lat, lon) AS location, mw FROM plants` turns coordinates into points; joins (`JOIN … USING (fips)`), filters and sums (`GROUP BY`) work too. **Spatial functions** work as well — areas, distances, projections, spatial joins: e.g. `SELECT name, ST_Area_Spheroid(ST_FlipCoordinates(ST_GeomFromGeoJSON(geometry))) / 1e6 AS area_km2 FROM counties`. The first one you use downloads the spatial add-on, so it takes a moment; a transform that uses it shows **uses spatial**, and opening its workspace later loads the add-on ahead of time. Any shape a query makes comes out as a geometry column you can draw. Save keeps your transforms with the workspace.
- **Profile** — a dataset's **Edit** shows, for each column, its type, how full it is, how many distinct values it has, and its range.
- **Export** — download any dataset as GeoJSON, Esri JSON or CSV for ArcGIS. Vectors become two number columns (east and north), which ArcGIS can draw as arrows.

## Common Questions

**Q: Why is my change not showing?**

A: This is a progressive web app (PWA), which means it caches content locally to work offline. If you deployed a new version but see an old one, try tapping "Reload latest" in the footer or opening the app in a private/incognito tab.

**Q: Can I use this offline?**

A: Mostly. Once you've opened the app, the app itself is cached. The background map tiles come from the internet, so without a connection you'll see the counties without the street map behind them.

**Q: How do I report a bug?**

A: Take a screenshot and share it with the developer. Tap **View logs** in the footer, then **Copy log**, and include that too — it helps debug issues.

**Q: What data is stored on my device?**

A: Only the app's cached files. Files you import stay in your browser and are not uploaded anywhere.
- **Saving** — the bar at the top shows your workspace's name (tap to rename) and whether it has **Unsaved changes**. Tap **Save** to keep them, or **Discard** to go back to the last save. Unsaved changes survive a reload on this device. Added datasets are stored right away; Save keeps which datasets the workspace uses, its visualizations and their order, and datasets' new names and descriptions (until then they show as unsaved changes, and Discard puts the old ones back). Today the database is in this browser, so nothing leaves the device; private browsing may not keep anything.
- **Assistant** — **✦ Assistant** at the top opens a chat with an AI helper (Claude). Ask for something to be shown or prepared, like "the latest average home price per county, and per state": it looks through your datasets, writes transforms and sets up visualizations, and you watch them appear in the graph and on the map. Its changes are unsaved until you **Save** (or **Discard** them). It may change or delete only **Local** datasets, never cloud ones. It starts from the datasets in your workspace (add the ones you care about first) and tells you how well they fit. When it needs something from you it shows a small card: a plan to review and edit, options to pick (one or several, or your own answer), and buttons for each way forward. It looks at your other datasets, or on the web, only if you agree. **On the web** (signed in): a **scout** finds out what data exists and how to get it, and a **scraper** fetches it into new datasets; you approve each run's brief and its spending cap (a run usually costs a few cents and takes a few minutes), watch its steps in the chat, and can stop it. If the page reloads mid-run, the run carries on and its results still arrive. Tap a step (✓ Query, ✓ Update the workspace…) to see exactly what it did, and 💭 **Reasoning** to read how it is thinking about your request; **Stop** halts it. On a computer it opens as a column on the right (drag its edge to resize); on a phone, as a sheet. Paste a screenshot into the message box, or attach one with 📎, to show it what you mean. **New chat** clears the conversation (it asks first; your workspace stays). Pick a **profile** in ⚙ too: it decides which model does each job — the chat, the scout and the scraper (and, soon, a reviewer). **Quick** (all Haiku) is the default and cheapest; **Smart scout** and **Deep research** put stronger models on finding data; **Planner** puts Opus on the chat. Under each profile you see its models and, once you've used it, its average cost per chat on this device. A chat keeps the profile it started with, so a new choice applies from the next chat. It needs your own Anthropic API key (⚙ in the panel): the key stays on this device, goes only to Anthropic, and usage is billed to your Anthropic account, so set a spending limit there.
- **Workspace** — the **Workspace** button at the top (next to Help) turns amber with a **●** when you have unsaved changes. Tap it to rename the workspace, see what it holds, **Save** or **Discard changes**, and download, load or reset it.
- **Workspace file** — **Workspace → Download workspace (.zip)** saves the workspace's setup (which datasets, the transforms and their SQL, the visualizations and their styles) — not the data itself — as a `.powergis.zip` of small files: `workspace.json`, `datasets.json`, each transform's settings and its SQL as its own `.sql` file, each visualization's settings, and a readable `overview.md`. You can unzip it, read or edit the SQL in any text editor, zip the folder again and load it back. **Load workspace file** opens such a zip (or an older `.powergis.json`, or a copied workspace saved as a file) into this workspace; datasets that aren't in the database are left out, and it tells you. Save to keep what you loaded. **Copy workspace** puts the same setup on the clipboard as text, and **Paste workspace…** opens a box (filled from the clipboard when your browser allows it; Clear and paste again if it's not what you meant), then **Load**.
- **Sample workspace** — **Workspace → Open the sample workspace** replaces this workspace's setup with the sample (Save to keep it, Discard to go back); your datasets stay under ＋ Dataset → Saved.
